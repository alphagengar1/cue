1. What we are building

The idea in one paragraph

Cue is an app for Even Realities G2 smart glasses that gives quiet networking help at in-person events such as career fairs, mixers, conferences, hackathon sponsor tables and alumni nights. You meet someone, hold the temple touchpad, and quietly say "Priya, Anduril, recruiter". A few seconds later three short cards appear in your line of sight. Then the display goes quiet so you can actually talk to the person.

The live moment

This is the only flow that must work end to end at the demo.

  1. You read a name badge or hear an introduction.
  2. You hold the temple touchpad (long press) and say the name, the org and the role. Releasing ends the capture.
  3. The glasses show "Listening", then "Looking up..." along with a "Heard:" line that echoes what Cue understood, so you can catch a speech mistake and ask again.
  4. Card 1 of 3 appears. Tap or swipe to flip through the cards.
  5. If Cue is unsure which person you mean, it first asks "Which Priya?" with up to 3 candidates plus "Neither (company only)".
  6. After you finish reading (or after 25 seconds with no input) the display drops to one dim status line. The screen is never fully black.
  7. Later, if the slow research finds something better, a dim "updated" marker appears. New content never pops over a live conversation.
  8. Double tap opens the system exit dialog. This is required by Even review.

Speed target: cards on screen in under 5 seconds from release. If the company is already preloaded, its card should show in under 2 seconds while person research continues. These targets are unverified until we measure on the venue wifi.

The three cards

CardPurposeWhat goes on it
1 ConnectAn opening line that is not small talkFirst that applies: a past conversation (later phase), shared background (school, past employer, club), shared interest, or a question about something they made or said.
2 ContextWho they are and what they and their org focus on nowTheir role and team, one or two recent public items, and the org's current focus from the preloaded event data.
3 Your angleHow this conversation serves your goalOne line linking your profile to them, plus one concrete ask, for example "Ask: is the fall SWE intern req still open?"

When Cue is not sure of the person, it says so rather than guessing. A strong match gets all 3 cards with sources. An uncertain match gets the confirm prompt first. No person match gets the org card only, starting with "No public info found for the name". If even the org is unknown, you get a generic goal-based ask labeled "Low info". Cue never invents a role or history.

Goals

The wearer picks one goal per event on the setup page, or switches it from the glasses menu (for example "Goal: Cofounder"). The goal mostly changes card 3. The goals are internship, cofounder, learn, teammates_sponsors and mentorship.

Event typeTypical goalExample ask on card 3
Career fairinternship"Which teams are hiring interns for summer?"
Mixercofounder"What are you building next? Looking for a technical partner?"
Conferencelearn"How did you handle X in your talk's system?"
Hackathon sponsor tablesteammates_sponsors"What would make a submission stand out for your track?"

Demo beat: switch from internship to cofounder for the same person. Card 3 changes and cards 1 and 2 stay exactly the same.

Quiet mode

Quiet mode is the default after you have read a brief. The HUD shows a single dim line such as "cue | Priya Raman | hold to ask". It stays calm even if a background update arrives (it only changes to "cue | 1 update | hold to ask"). This protects the conversation and keeps us inside Even's rule against a black screen.

What we do not do

  • No face recognition. The G2 has no camera, and identity comes only from what the wearer says.
  • No recording of conversations. Audio is captured only while push-to-talk is held, and it is discarded after transcription.
  • No always-on listening.
  • No LinkedIn scraping. Cue never fetches LinkedIn page contents.
  • No meeting prep for scheduled calendar meetings. Cue is for the walk-up moment.
  • No covert use. Cue helps you remember and prepare; it is not a way to pretend you know someone.

Later and optional: a follow-up draft via Photon iMessage, memory of past conversations, and an end-of-event summary. None of these are needed for the demo.

2. How it works

The components in plain words

  • Glasses app. A small TypeScript web app (Vite) that runs inside the Even Realities phone app and draws on the G2 through the Even Hub SDK. It records push-to-talk audio, sends it to our backend, and shows the cards. It holds no API keys.
  • Backend. One Node/Express server on Vultr. It owns every secret and does all the work: speech-to-text, store lookup, web search, the brief-writing model call, CREAO runs, and later Photon. It uses SQLite for events, orgs, people, profile and briefs.
  • Setup page. A plain web page served by the backend, used on a laptop to paste the wearer profile, pick the goal, load the event, and run typed lookups as a demo fallback.
  • Third-party services. Deepgram, Exa, Claude Haiku 5.5, CREAO agents and Sentry (details below).

The app and backend talk over plain HTTPS requests with polling, not WebSockets, because WebSockets drop when the phone backgrounds the app and polling recovers on its own. The backend is single-tenant for the weekend: one wearer, one profile, one active event.

Two paths

  • Fast path (seconds). Audio, then speech-to-text, then a store lookup and web search, then one model call that writes the cards. This must work without CREAO.
  • Slow path (minutes). CREAO agents research the person more deeply in the background. When a run finishes, the backend builds an improved brief and the glasses pick it up as "updated".

CREAO also does the "load the event" job the night before. That preloaded data is what makes the fast path fast, which is why CREAO is load-bearing and not a side feature.

The live path, step by step

  1. Long press on the touchpad. The app turns on the microphone and shows "Listening".
  2. Release. The app stops audio after a short 300 ms tail and shows "Looking up...".
  3. The app sends the audio to POST /api/stt. The backend wraps it as a WAV file, sends it to Deepgram with the event's names as hints, and returns the transcript plus a parsed name, org and role.
  4. The glasses show "Heard: Priya | Anduril | recruiter".
  5. The app sends the transcript to POST /api/briefs/text.
  6. The backend matches the org against the event store, and in parallel runs the Exa searches and loads the wearer profile and goal.
  7. The backend filters results: a result is kept only if it mentions both the name and the org, and anything from linkedin.com is dropped.
  8. One call to Claude Haiku 5.5 writes the 3 cards as JSON. The backend checks the JSON, runs the safety filter, trims to the card limits, and returns Brief version 1.
  9. The glasses show card 1.
  10. In the background the backend starts a CREAO Person Researcher run (only for a confirmed or strong match). When it finishes, a Brief Writer run merges the findings into Brief version 2, which the app picks up by polling GET /api/briefs/:id?since=.

Which services and why

The three CREAO agents

  • Event Loader. Runs the night before (or when you click Load event on the setup page, which calls POST /api/events). Input is the event's org list, people and pasted source text. Output is Organization records and seed Person records.
  • Person Researcher. Runs after a strong match or after the wearer picks a person on the confirm screen. Finds sourced professional facts and a match level.
  • Brief Writer. Runs after the Person Researcher and produces Brief version 2.

The backend starts runs at developer.creao.ai/v1/runs with a webhook to /webhooks/creao, and also polls every 15 seconds as a backup. A run still pending after 15 minutes is marked failed. To save credits, there is at most one Person Researcher run per person per event, and none for low matches. The backend re-checks everything the agents return; it does not trust them blindly.

3. What the glasses show

The display in brief

  • 576 x 288 pixels, monochrome green, one fixed font. No bold, no sizes, no emoji.
  • One layout created once: three stacked text containers named hdr (header), body and foot (footer). Every change after startup is an in-place text update, so there is no flicker.
  • Brightness is a level from 0 to 4. Quiet text uses a low level, but never level 0 for the only visible line.
  • Safe characters are ASCII plus a few box glyphs. Use "|" as a separator.
  • The line budget (about 40 characters per line, about 5 body lines) is unverified until we measure on hardware.

What each gesture does

GestureEffect

Push-to-talk has two modes behind one setting called PTT_MODE. "hold" is preferred (long press to start, release to stop). "tapToggle" is the fallback (tap to start, tap again or 8 seconds to stop). We pick one at the first hardware test, because we do not yet know that a plain long press reaches the app. The menu item Talk works in both modes.

Card writing rules

  • Hard limits: header and footer 40 characters on one line. Body 200 characters, at most 5 lines of 40 characters. Aim for 90 to 130. The app truncates with "..." if exceeded.
  • ASCII plus the safe glyphs only. Everything else is stripped before sending.
  • At most 2 points per card, and each card ends with something the wearer can say or ask.
  • Lead with the fact ("Both FIRST Robotics alums", not "It looks like you both..."). First names only in the body.
  • Digits for numbers, short months (Sep). Allowed abbreviations: SWE, PM, ML, AI, eng, univ., yrs, intro, Q1 to Q4.
  • No URLs in the body (the source domain goes in the footer), no markdown, no emoji, no hedging such as "might". If unsure, leave it out.
  • Professional, self-published information only. Never family, home, health or politics.
  • Connect: one shared thread plus one question about their work. Context: role and tenure, then the org's focus. Your angle: link your goal to this person, then the specific ask.

4. Research and safety rules

Sources

Allowed sources are public, professional and ideally self-published: talks, podcasts and interviews; their own posts, blogs, personal site or GitHub; articles about their work; school, team and company pages; and search result titles and snippets (including a LinkedIn headline shown in a result).

The Person Researcher prefers sources in this priority order, and cards prefer the highest available:

  1. Talks and podcasts they appeared on
  2. Their own posts and blogs
  3. Personal site or GitHub
  4. Articles about them
  5. School or team pages

Every person-specific claim must carry a source, and the card footer shows the source domain. A connect or context card with no source is dropped. If sources conflict, prefer the self-published one and lower the match level.

Disambiguation: is this the right person?

A search result is kept only if its title or text mentions both the person's name and the org (or an alias). This is enforced in backend code, not only in the prompt.

Match levelWhenWhat the wearer sees
highA stored Person matches name and org, or 2 or more kept results are about one personAll 3 cards
mediumExactly 1 kept result, or results name 2 or more different full names, or the wearer said only a first name that cannot be expanded to one full nameThe Confirm person picker first, then 3 cards
lowNo kept resultsOne org Context card starting "No public info found for the name"

Name expansion: if the wearer says only a first name and the store has exactly one person at that org with that first name, Cue uses the full name for search. Otherwise the match is capped at medium.

Privacy

  • Never use or show family, relationships, home address or location, health, politics, religion, age, appearance, or anything from a login-gated page.
  • LinkedIn: we never fetch or scrape linkedin.com pages. The fast path excludes the domain at search time, and the backend drops any fact whose source is linkedin.com.
  • A code blocklist (words like wife, husband, kids, married, address, diagnosis, church, democrat, republican) drops sensitive facts as a backstop to the model prompt.
  • Audio is held in memory for transcription and then discarded. It is never written to disk, logs or Sentry.
  • The brief (cards, sources, transcript text) is kept on our backend for the event so updates and goal switching work, and on the wearer's device. We delete backend briefs after the event.
  • The privacy policy must cover the microphone and network permissions and list our backend domains. This is required for Even review.
  • Demo consent: we only demo on people who consented and have a public bio.
  • Honesty line for the pitch: "Person lookup is real web search. We preloaded the demo event with people who have public bios."
